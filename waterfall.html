💧 Waterfall Model

Sequential development approach

Description

The Waterfall model is a linear and sequential approach to software development. Each phase must be completed entirely before the next phase begins, with no overlap between phases. The process flows steadily downwards (like a waterfall) through distinct stages.

The typical phases include: Requirements Analysis, System Design, Implementation, Testing, Deployment, and Maintenance. Each phase has specific deliverables and review processes before moving to the next stage.

This model emphasizes thorough documentation at each stage and works best when requirements are well-understood from the beginning.

✅ Advantages

  • • Simple and easy to understand: Clear structure makes it easy for teams to follow and manage.
  • • Well-documented: Each phase produces deliverables and documentation, ensuring thorough records.
  • • Easy to manage: Distinct phases with specific deliverables make project management straightforward.
  • • Works well for small projects: Ideal when requirements are clear and unlikely to change.
  • • Clear milestones: Easy to track progress with defined phase completion criteria.

❌ Disadvantages

  • • Inflexible to changes: Difficult and expensive to go back to previous phases if requirements change.
  • • No working software until late: Users don't see the product until implementation is complete.
  • • High risk and uncertainty: Problems may not be discovered until testing phase, which can be costly.
  • • Not suitable for complex projects: Real-world projects often have evolving requirements that waterfall cannot accommodate.
  • • Poor model for long projects: Requirements may become outdated by the time the project is completed.

🎯 When to Use Waterfall

✓ Ideal For:

  • • Well-defined, stable requirements
  • • Short-term projects with fixed scope
  • • Projects requiring extensive documentation
  • • Regulated industries (healthcare, finance)
  • • Projects with fixed budget and timeline

✗ Avoid For:

  • • Unclear or evolving requirements
  • • Long, complex projects
  • • Projects needing frequent client feedback
  • • Innovative or experimental products
  • • Projects requiring rapid deployment
